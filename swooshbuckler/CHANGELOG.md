# Swooshbuckler changelog

## Unreleased

- **Shortcuts you can see and change.** Settings now lists every action Swooshbuckler can do with the key it has: click a row and press the key you want, Esc leaves it as it was and Backspace takes the key away. A key another action already has is only moved once you say so, and **Reset To Defaults** puts them all back. Your keys are kept with the rest of the settings. There are keys for the rolls ([ and ]), for keeping one (K), for stepping through the presets (, and .), for the morph pad (M), the cables (C), Takes, Render Variations and Settings, as well as the ones that were there before.
- **The key is shown wherever the action is**: beside the item in the menus, at the end of the control's tooltip, and - in the app on a Mac - in a menu bar of its own (File, Edit, Transport and View). No control spells a key out in its own words any more, so a key you change reads the same everywhere, including the hint under Swell, the sea's tooltip and the line along the foot of the window.
- **Tooltips come in two parts.** Rest on a control and it says its name and what it is set to out of what it can be; stay a moment longer and it grows into the whole explanation, with how to handle it and its key at the end. The how-to line that used to be repeated on every knob is now written once.
- **One clock for everything that moves**, stepped with the real time of the frame, so nothing runs slow when the window is busy and no spring is thrown across the screen by a late frame. Sound still answers instantly; the interface eases, and never for longer than a quarter of a second.
- **The tape's reels spin up and coast down** instead of starting and stopping dead, the record lamp breathes while the tape is armed and waiting (and is solid while it writes), and Last 60 s flicks the reels back half a turn.
- **Each track's colour stripe breathes with its ride**, so the left edge of the track list reads as a row of lanterns.
- **A wire clicks in.** The plug drops onto its knob and settles, and the knob's ring flashes in the wire's colour.
- **A preset or a roll passes through the window as a wave**: the knobs set off left to right, row by row, over a quarter of a second, instead of all at once.
- **The die tumbles** through three faces when you roll it, then settles.
- **What the rig says back is said where you can see it**: a plate at the top of the open water that rises into place and fades after a couple of seconds. The footer keeps the same line as a log.
- **The web address and the version in the header are quieter**, so the transport and the tape read first.

- **The sea is the hero, and it moves properly.** It runs on the screen's own frame clock, up to sixty times a second however fast the screen is, and the swell rolls away smoothly instead of stepping. Rows go colder and hazier towards the horizon, the near crests are drawn as curves, and the water has its own colours so nothing on it can be mistaken for a bus. The sky is kept in one picture and painted again only when the view changes shape, and after five seconds of quiet the sea steps at a third of the rate.
- **The sea answers what it hears.** A pass leaves a bump in the water under it that rolls away with the swell; a hit fires a short cherry glow along the horizon and a ring on the water; a click drops a ripple where it landed; holding Swell raises the swell; and the moon's halo warms and widens while the limiter is holding the output down. A thrown whoosh is the same comet in brass, and brighter, labelled Throw.
- **The moon moved to the left of the sky**, with its glitter path on the water below it, clear of the Morph button and the readouts.
- **The mark in the stars.** Every so often, and whenever a take is put away, brighter stars pick out the outline of the logo in the sky, joined by faint lines. It twinkles for a few seconds and fades away. It replaces the outline that used to trace itself after a silence.
- **Comet labels keep their distance** from the panel's name, the recording lamp, the Morph button and each other.
- **One recording time.** The sea shows a blinking cherry lamp and the word Rec; the time is on the tape's own button in the header.
- **An empty sea says what to do.** With no sounds loaded it is calm, with one line of hint.
- **The morph pad docks** over the lower right of the sea on a plate of its own, with the sea dimmed behind it, instead of covering the whole picture. Two thirds of the sea stay in view.
- **A landscape window, with the tracks and the sea on top.** The window is now 1520 x 900. Tracks and the sea sit side by side across the top two fifths, and the sea is over three times the size it was. Under them are Ride, Bus A and Bus B in one row, then Hits and Output, then Modulation along the bottom. Every control is still on the one screen.
- **Knobs line up in columns.** Bus A, Bus B and the Hits share one knob spacing, and the Hits' seven knobs sit directly under bus A's.
- **The header is in three groups**, with a thin rule between them: transport (Follow Host, Play, New), presets (Undo, Redo, the preset box, A / B, Randomise, its history and the star) and tape (the tape, Last 60 s, Variations, Takes). Settings is at the far right.
- **Scope strips.** What Bus A, Bus B, the Hits and the Output send on is drawn in a strip the width of the panel along its bottom, in place of the small waveform in the title bar.
- **New: the ride's last 8 seconds** in the Ride panel: where the ride has had bus A's faders (coral) and bus B's (turquoise).
- **Ride is laid out again**: Swell at the left, the knobs in a row with Throw Length at the end, Throw under its Throw Length knob, and Follow Master beside Auto Ride.
- **Hits is laid out again**: the gate light, Aim, Sync Hits and the Gate and Duck boxes are together at the left of the panel.
- **Track lanes are coloured by their bus**: coral on Bus A, turquoise on Bus B, cherry on the Hits, with each further track on the same bus a step lighter or darker. The bus tag carries the bus's letter (A, B, or H for the Hits) on a small plate. The morph's ropes take the same colours.
- **Four whole tracks are always in view.** With more than four, the list scrolls a track at a time and never shows half a lane. Once tracks are loaded, the drop zone is one slim line under them.
- **A saved take is announced in the Tracks title bar**, in brass, and a click on the message opens Takes.
- **Modulators are laid out the same way whatever their type**, and each says what it is wired to ("1 wire - Bus B Swirl"). Cables now leave their jack straight up, out of the top of the card.
- **Bus B's Ducked and Filtered** sit in a tinted group marked From Bus A, and while Link is on its Doppler and Width read Linked To A.
- **Peak Tremolo** has its switch and name along the top of its group, its two menus at the left and its three knobs beside them.
- **The Morph button sits in the sea's top right**, and the moon has moved across to the left of the sky, so neither is in the other's way.
- **Window size.** The first time, the window opens at its full size or the largest that fits whole on your screen. It can be made smaller down to 85% and larger up to 200%.
- **A new knob.** Every knob is a chunky, flat ship's wheel with six spokes, one of them a sand pointer you can't miss, inside a ring that fills with the value in its section's colour. There are two sizes on the whole screen, and the rows in Bus A, Bus B and the Hits sit on shared columns.
- **Every knob shows its value all the time**, under its name, in its own units ("6.4 s", "+3.0 dB", "35%"). It updates as you drag. Typing a value (right-click or Alt-double-click) and double-click to reset work as before.
- **The wheel turns with your hand.** While you drag, the wheel is exactly where the value is; let go while turning and it coasts on a touch before it settles. Presets and Randomise still swing the wheels in.
- **Modulation rings.** Each wire on a knob is a thin ring outside the value ring, in its modulator's colour; more wires stack outwards, a negative depth is dashed, and a dot shows where the modulation has the value right now.
- **Tidier cables.** Plugs sit on the right of the wheel's rim, never on a knob's name or value, and each cable comes up beside the text and turns in to its plug. Put the mouse on a modulator's card or on one of its plugs and its cables stand out while the others fade.
- **Meters with numbers.** GR and Out are slotted windows with their names above and a number under each: the deepest recent reduction and the highest recent peak, in dB. The Out bar turns brass above −6 dB and cherry for the last decibel. The Trim fader has a scale beside it and its setting under it.
- **Swell is a flat brass boss** with eight block handles, still the biggest thing in the Ride panel, with Throw right under it.
- **Hard edges everywhere.** Nothing in the window has a rounded corner: panels, buttons, menus, tags, text boxes, meters, fader caps, popup menus and the icons are all square-cornered, with sharp joins on their outlines. Only things that are round in life stay round: the wheels, the Swell button, bolts, lamps, jacks, ropes and cables, the moon and the stars.
- **A new finish for the whole window: stamped ship's plates.** Every panel is a flat iron plate with hard, square corners, a pressed edge and a slotted bolt in each corner. Buttons, menus and tags are smaller plates set into it with their names stamped in, and scopes, waveforms, meters and text boxes are windows cut into the plate. Nothing is a pill and nothing has round corners except the things that are round on a ship: the wheels, the bolts, the lamps and the jacks. One rope runs under the header. The sea is a rectangular window in an iron frame with a bolted plate in each corner.
- **One way of writing names.** Every control, button, menu and tag is in Title Case ("Skip Silence", "Auto Ride", "Last 60 s", "Peak Tremolo") and every hint and message is a sentence. Nothing is in capitals any more except single letters (A, B, M, S) and acronyms (GR, LFO, MIDI), and the same name is written the same way in the window, the tooltips, the messages and this manual. A few names changed their small words to match: **One File per Whoosh**, **Match Pitch to Master**, **Show in Finder**.
- **Text always fits.** Every label, value and message is measured before it is drawn: it is set a little smaller when it has to be, never smaller than a readable size, and only a name of yours (a file, a preset, a track) is ever cut, in its middle so its start and its take number both stay. The smallest text in the window is larger than before, the faint text is easier to read, and the meter scales are full size.
- **Buttons, chips, menus, switches and faders each have one look.** A button is an iron plate with a dark lip under it; switched on, the whole plate turns brass, or its section's colour, with dark lettering, and one that switches something carries a small square lamp slot that fills in. Play is the one brass key. **M** and **S** are small tags. Menus have a drawn arrow. A switch is a slot with a block that slides across, and **Peak Tremolo** is one, as are the choices in Settings that are on or off. Faders have a rectangular block cap in a slot, and the Output meters are slots cut in the plate.
- **Drawn icons** for the things that were letters: the cross that removes a track, the arrows either side of the preset name, the play and stop glyphs, the die on Randomise, the sliders on Settings and the page on New.
- **Three ways a control can be out of play, each drawn differently.** Greyed out: it can't be used now (the gate's knobs while Aim is on). Hollow, with a dashed arc or edge: it can be set but does nothing until something else is switched on (Peak Tremolo's knobs while it is off, Energy with Auto Ride off, Follow Master without a Master). A chain link in the other bus's colour: it is following another control (bus B's Doppler, Width and Pass while Link is on).
- **One accent colour.** The two golds are now one brass, used for everything that is not a section. Bus A is coral, Bus B turquoise, the Hits cherry, and the four modulators have colours of their own (lilac, sky blue, rose and lime) that are none of those.
- **Each panel's title sits on a nameplate** riveted to the panel, with a tally in the section's colour at its left: a letter for Bus A and Bus B, a burst for the Hits, a picture for the others. The bus tag on each track is a small square plate in its bus's colour.
- **The tape's time moved** to the left of the sea, beside its name, so it no longer sits under the Morph button; the names over the comets step aside while the morph pad is open.
- **The scrollbar in Tracks, the Trim fader and the corner you resize by** are drawn in the same style as everything else.
- **Tooltips**: every one now starts with the control's name.
- **The line over each waveform** (its length, its sounds, its file, or which hit is aimed) sits on a dark plate, so it reads over a loud sound and over the hit marks.

- **New: Load Demo Sounds.** A button in the empty Tracks panel fills four tracks with sounds made on the spot: a growl (bus A), a stereo wind bed and air swishes (bus B) and four hits. Press Play and you have a whoosh to shape before you have loaded anything of your own. One Undo takes them out again.
- **New: keyboard shortcuts.** Return is Play / Stop, R is Randomise, T arms and stops the tape and L saves the last minute, alongside F for Throw, the space bar for Swell and Cmd+Z / Shift+Cmd+Z for Undo and Redo. They never fire while you are typing in a text box or while a panel is open.
- **New: type a value.** Right-click a knob (or Alt-double-click it) and type a value in its own units, such as "-12 dB", "4.2 s" or "35%"; Return keeps it, Esc cancels. It works on every knob, the track faders, Semitones, Cents and Master Trim, and a typed value is one Undo step. Alt-click on a knob no longer resets it (double-click does).
- **New: Learn MIDI CC.** Right-click a track's fader or the Energy knob, choose Learn MIDI CC and move a fader or knob on a MIDI controller: it moves that control from then on. Nothing is mapped until you do, and the mappings are kept in the settings.
- **The file name is shown on each track**, after its length and its count of sounds in the waveform (cut in the middle when it is long) and in the track name's tooltip.
- **New: Licences in About**, showing the notices of the software and typefaces Swooshbuckler is built with. They are also in THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md.
- **The README has a fuller In a DAW section** (which kind of track, what MIDI notes do, Follow Host, automation, where recordings go) and lists every shortcut.
- **Plain words in the track tooltips**: Remove now says what it does.
- **A smaller app.** An unused graphics library is no longer built in. Nothing looks or sounds different.
- **New: stereo beds.** A stereo file used to be folded to mono when it was loaded, which is right for a doppler pass but narrowed wide ambiences. A stereo file whose two sides differ now keeps its width, and a stereo sound on Bus B with Pass set to Bed plays wide: as wide as the file at Doppler 0%, folding into the moving point as Doppler rises, and scaled by Width. Bus A, the hits, thrown whooshes and Fly-By passes still play the mono sum, and so does a track with Vari off or a sound being morphed. Mono files and older sessions sound exactly as before. A stereo sound takes about twice the memory of a mono one, and sessions store the extra only for sounds that have it.
- **New: the Master track.** A star on each lane makes that track the Master, the one sound the others can follow (one at a time). Swooshbuckler then works out how the sound moves over its length: its loudness, its pitch and its brightness. Three things use it. **Followers** can follow **Master**, **Master Pitch** or **Master Brightness**, so any knob moves with the Master sound as it plays. **Match Pitch To Master** (right-click a lane) sets a pitched track's Semitones and Cents so it sits on the Master's pitch, to the nearest cent. **Follow Master**, a new button in the Ride panel, makes every other track's ride swell with the Master's loudness instead of its own slow wave; Energy still sets how deep the dips go.
- **New: choose a track's bus.** The bus tag on a lane is now a menu: **Auto** (by sound type, the default), **Bus A**, **Bus B** or **Hits**. A bus chosen by hand is marked ` *`, and the track keeps its type and its name. Saved with the session.
- **New: morph ropes.** Drag the rope from the A or B handle under the morph pad onto a track and that side of the morph is that one track, not its whole bus; the rest of the bus plays on unmorphed. Rope one side or both. With the morph off a roped track plays through its bus as usual. Double-click a handle to let the rope go. Ropes are saved with the session and let go when their track is removed.
- **The Master, the buses you chose and the ropes can all be undone**, and Render Variations renders with them.
- **A modulator's Follows menu is longer** (Master, Master Pitch and Master Brightness come after Output). If a DAW project automates that menu itself, check that lane: saved sessions and projects open with the same choice as before.
- **New: your own presets.** **Save Preset...** in the preset list saves the settings as they are under a name, and they appear under **Your Presets** after the factory ones, with **Rename Preset...**, **Delete Preset** and **Show Preset Folder**. Tick **Include Modulators and Wires** to store those too. Each preset is a small `.swooshpreset` file in a `Swooshbuckler Presets` folder beside the settings: send the file to share it, drop one on the window to load it. **<** and **>** step through your presets as well.
- **Randomise leaves every other knob where it is.** A roll used to put every control it didn't roll back to its default, so Hold, Floor, Return, Width, Bend and Throw Length were reset each time. Now only the rolled knobs move. Width and Bend have joined the roll, within safe ranges (Width 35 to 100%, Bend 30 to 90%). Factory presets are still complete looks that reset what they don't set, and the tooltip says so.
- **New: a history for Randomise.** Two arrows beside Randomise step back and forward through the last 20 rolls and the settings you had before the first of them; each comes back as you left it. A star keeps the current roll as a preset of your own.
- **New: section dice and locks.** Ride, Bus A, Bus B, Hits and Output each have a die that rolls just that section, and a padlock that keeps the section out of the header's Randomise (and out of Randomise Everything in Render Variations). The locks are saved with the session.
- **New: Undo and Redo for everything.** Two arrows in the header, and Cmd+Z / Shift+Cmd+Z wherever you last clicked in the window, take back knob moves, presets, Randomise rolls, section dice, A / B, New and track edits, in the order you made them. A preset or a roll is one step however many knobs glide. New is one step too: Undo brings back the tracks, the settings and the wires.
- **New: A / B.** An A / B button in the header holds two sets of the settings a preset owns; click to switch, and **Copy to Other** in the preset list copies across. Both sets are saved with the session.
- **New: session files.** **Save Session As...**, **Save Session**, **Open Session...** and **Recent Sessions** in the preset list (Cmd+S, Shift+Cmd+S and Cmd+O in the app) keep a whole session, audio included, in one `.swooshsession` file. The app's window title shows the session's name, and a session file dropped on the window opens. The app and the plug-in read each other's files.
- **Sessions are about a quarter smaller.** The saved state (session files, the app's own copy and what a DAW project stores) is now written in a compact form: a session with 7 seconds of audio went from 1167 KB to 877 KB. Sessions and projects saved before still open.
- **A long preset name is never cut short** in the preset box: it is set a little smaller to fit.
- **New: Render Variations.** A Variations button in the header opens a panel that renders a batch of whooshes straight to files, in the background and much faster than real time (100 whooshes of 3 seconds in about 20 seconds). Choose the **Count** (1 to 1000, 100 unless you change it), the **Length** of each whoosh, and **What Varies**: **Randomise Everything** (a fresh Randomise roll per file), **Small Changes** (the settings as they are, nudged by up to **Amount**, with each track starting a little further in) or **Seed Only** (the same settings, different passes). Each file is one thrown whoosh rendered from silence until its tail has rung out, through the same output stage as the tape and in the tape's format, named `<Name>_001` onwards in a dated folder of its own (or a folder you choose, where numbers carry on and nothing is written over). A progress bar shows the percentage, "37 of 100", the file in hand and the time left; **Cancel** keeps the files already rendered and leaves nothing half-written. The rig keeps playing while a batch renders, and the header button shows how far it is. The same **Batch Seed** renders the same batch again, and every file's description lists the settings it was made with. The rendered files join Takes.

- **New: One File Per Whoosh** (in Settings, off by default). The armed tape closes its file each time a whoosh has ended and opens the next for the next one, so a session of riding leaves a folder of separate, numbered whooshes. A whoosh has ended when the level has sat about 25 dB under its peaks for three quarters of a second, or when a thrown whoosh has rung out with the rig stopped. Sounds with less than a fifth of a second of level in them are not kept.
- **New: Takes.** A Takes button in the header, with a count, opens the last 20 recordings of this run: tape takes, whoosh files and Last 60 s saves, each with a small waveform, its name and its length. Click one to hear it (it plays over the rig and is not recorded), drag it out to the Finder, Explorer or a DAW track, show it in its folder, star it to keep it in the list, or bin it to the system trash. Clicking a "Saved ..." message now opens Takes.
- **Markers in WAV takes.** Takes carry cue points with labels at every doppler pass peak (Pass A, Pass B), every thrown whoosh's peak (Throw) and every aimed, synced or gated hit (Hit), so a long take arrives already spotted in tools that read WAV cues.
- **File names you can use.** Recordings are named after the preset, or a **Take Name** you set in Settings, with a four-digit counter that carries on while Swooshbuckler is open: `Jet Passes_0007.wav`. **File Names** in Settings switches to the date and time form.
- **Every file says how it was made.** WAV, AIFF and FLAC recordings all carry a description (version, preset and the main settings) and a **Creator**, which you can set in Settings.
- **Last 60 s can keep 3 or 5 minutes.** **Keep the Last** in Settings sets it. The sound is held in memory: about 23 MB a minute at 48 kHz, 92 MB a minute at 192 kHz.
- **A take with no sound leaves no file.** Arming the tape and stopping it before anything played used to leave an empty file behind.
- **New: Throw.** A Throw button in the Ride panel (and the F key) fires exactly one whoosh: a single pass on both buses with a long approach, a peak and a shorter tail, and with Aim on the hits land on its peak. While the rig plays it goes on top of the ride. With the rig stopped you get just that one whoosh and then silence, so single whooshes can be made one at a time. The peak is heard half a Throw Length after you throw (within a few thousandths of a second), and the top of the Ride panel counts down to it ("Peak in 1.2 s"). The new **Throw Length** knob sets how long it lasts, from 0.3 to 6 seconds, or follows Rate.
- **New: Keys.** A Keys menu in the Ride panel sets what MIDI notes do. **Swell**: any held note holds Swell, as before. **Pitch**: the held notes set the pitch, each as its distance from middle C added to the Pitch knob; a chord is dealt out over the tracks in order, lowest note to the first track, so each layer gets its own pitch, and letting go glides back. **Throw**: every note throws one whoosh at that note's pitch, with how hard you play setting how close and loud it passes. The notes you hold are shown at the top of the Ride panel.
- **The app listens to every MIDI keyboard.** MIDI inputs are opened when the app starts and when a keyboard is plugged in.
- **New in the plug-in: Follow Host.** On by default: the rig plays while the DAW's transport plays and stops, with its tail, when it stops. Every transport start begins the ride from the same place, so the same project bounces the same way each time. Switch it off in the header to start and stop only with Play.
- **Play can be automated**, and the play state is saved with a DAW project. The app still always opens stopped.
- **Bounces keep the tail.** The plug-in now tells the host how long its tail can ring (6.5 seconds).
- **Wander carries on with the window closed.** The morph dot's wander now runs inside the rig, so it keeps moving when the Swooshbuckler window is closed and is part of an offline bounce. It no longer moves the Morph X and Morph Y controls, so it writes no automation.
- **New wander shape: Butterfly**, the butterfly curve, at the end of the shape menu.
- **Fast doppler passes stay clean.** A pass that comes in fast plays the sound up to twice as fast, and the top of a bright sound used to fold back down as whistles sweeping the wrong way. What would go above the top of the audible range is now filtered out: a 20 kHz test tone through the fastest pass used to leave fold-back only 4 dB under the sound, and now leaves it 70 dB under. Bright sounds also keep a little more top end through every pass. The rig uses about 1% more of one processor core.
- **Play straight after Stop doesn't click.** Pressing Play while the sounds were still letting go (the first 60 ms after Stop) used to cut them mid-wave. They now fade out over 5 ms first, then start again.
- **Fly-By is as loud as Bed.** A bus on Fly-By used to be about 1 to 2 LU quieter than the same bus on Bed. Its passes are now 1.8 dB louder, which puts the two modes within half a LU of each other on steady and gappy sounds alike. The presets that use Fly-By (Jet Passes, Monster Swells, Fast and Furious) are that much louder.
- **Vari on and off are closer in level when pitched up.** With a track's Semitones up, Vari on now carries a small make-up (1 dB per octave). On a hissy sound the two settings used to differ by 2 to 2.4 LU at +7 and +12 semitones; they are now within 1.4 LU, and within 1.1 LU on tonal sounds.
- **The safety copy is only written when you change something.** With Wander on, the moving morph dot used to count as a change, so the session was saved every minute for as long as the app ran. Only your own edits count now. Dragging how deep a wire reaches counts as an edit too (it didn't before, so that change could be missing from the copy).
- **The tape stops rolling when the rig rests.** With Skip Silence on, the tape used to show as capturing from Stop until the next Play. It now stops once the tail has died away.
- **Undo uses about half the memory.** Each undo step in Tracks held the sound plus the extra copies the rig keeps for clean pitched-up playback (nearly as much again). A step now keeps only the sound and gets its copies back when you undo to it. The whole undo history is also limited to about 500 MB of audio: past that, the oldest edits are forgotten first, and the latest one can always be undone.
- **Replaced sounds are freed when the audio stops.** A sound you had replaced or removed could stay in memory for as long as the audio device was stopped. It is now let go at once.
- **Switching the morph on and off is clean.** The morph runs about 43 ms behind the buses, and its fade used to mix the live sound with that late copy, which was heard as a doubled sound for a third of a second. The fade now runs against a copy of the buses lined up with the morph, joined on with a 10 ms splice. Switching the morph on again no longer plays back a scrap of sound from the last time it was on.
- **The morph keeps the dynamics of both buses.** With the dot between A and B, the level now follows the mix of the two buses' loudness, so a swell or a hit on one bus comes through at nearly its full size (about 3 dB down in the middle of the pad). Before, a swell on the louder bus was pulled halfway down towards the quieter one (a swell 26 dB over the other bus came out 13 dB down in the middle). With the two buses at different levels, the middle of the pad is now louder than it was, and the level moves evenly from one side to the other in every mode.
- **Glide keeps attacks sharper.** When a hit arrives, the gliding partials line up on it and no longer smear ahead of it. With the dot near one side there is now nothing ahead of the hit; towards the middle of the pad there is about 5 dB less than before.
- **Vari off holds its level.** Noisy sounds used to come out 1 to 3 dB quieter through the pitch shifter, more the further the shift; a steady tone could move by up to 0.8 dB with the setting. Both now come out at the level they went in. Shifting up still loses whatever goes above the top of the audible range, as it must.
- **Hits with Vari off have no pre-echo.** A shifted hit used to be preceded by about 20 ms of its own sound, some 25 dB under it. Nothing of a hit is now heard before it starts.
- **Stop lets the tail ring out.** Pressing Stop lets go of the sounds and lets the Fill echoes, the passes in flight and Space die away, in your speakers and on the tape, for up to 6 seconds, then rests. A take used to end in a cut at full level 60 ms after Stop; now it ends with its tail. Play during the tail starts again at once.
- **New setting: Skip Silence** (in Settings, on by default). The armed tape now judges quiet against the recent peaks: it stops rolling once the output has sat about 25 dB under them for three quarters of a second, and starts again, a quarter second early, when the sound comes back, with a short fade at each join. Before, it only stopped under a fixed, very low level, which a playing rig never reaches. Deep, slow swells leave gaps for it to skip; a busy rig with a steady bed is still recorded almost whole. Switch it off and the tape rolls all the time, for one continuous take.
- **Last 60 s is written the way the tape is set**: the same File Type, Bit Depth, Sample Rate and Channels (it was always a stereo WAV at the rig's rate).
- **16-Bit recordings are dithered**, so quiet fades end in a faint steady hiss and not in grit.
- **More room for a slow disk at high sample rates.** The tape buffers about 3 seconds of audio at any rate before anything is lost (at 192 kHz it was under 0.7 s). If audio is lost, the message that names the saved file says how much, and the figure is now right at high sample rates.
- **The app keeps a safety copy of your session.** About once a minute when something has changed, and a few seconds after a track is loaded, edited or removed, the session is saved in the background without holding up the interface. If the app doesn't quit properly, the next launch opens that copy.
- **Tape settings changed in the app are kept.** A change to the recording folder, format or Auto-Arm made while the app was open could be undone when the app quit and saved the session. The app and the rig now share one settings file handler.
- **Recordings name their author.** The broadcast-wave details in WAV files give Samuel Justice as the originator reference.
- **The wander shape that traces the logo is called Cherries.** Sessions and presets that use it are unaffected.
- **New: Pass Mode.** Each bus has a Pass menu in its title bar. **Bed** is the two overlapped doppler passes as before: a steady level, best for beds and air. **Fly-By** plays one pass at a time, with a long approach, the peak at the closest point and a shorter tail, so a tone or a sound with a sharp start is heard once and at one pitch (in Bed the incoming and outgoing pass sound together about a third of the time; in Fly-By under a tenth, and far quieter). The passes peak at the same moments, so Aim, Link and the sea work the same; a linked bus B uses bus A's mode. Both buses start on Bed. The presets Jet Passes, Monster Swells and Fast and Furious now use Fly-By on bus A.
- **The passes move the colours when the faders are still.** With Auto Ride off, a low Energy, or Swell held, Low End, Swirl and Filter now follow how close each doppler pass is, not just the level (which hardly moves then). On a steady sound with Auto Ride off, the Filter and Swirl now sweep with every pass about as much as they do with the ride on, and the Low End lift comes down between passes. With the ride at its default depth or deeper, the sound is unchanged.
- **Auto Ride off and Swell no longer run hot.** With every fader up all the time the mix was about 4 dB louder into the limiter (which then squashed the peaks by 5 to 6 dB). The mix is now eased down to match the ridden level, by up to about 5 dB, so switching Auto Ride compares movement and not loudness, and Push and Inflate behave the same either way. A Swell is still a push: the level comes up at once and eases back over a couple of seconds if you keep holding. Settings with Energy at 35% or more sound as before; sessions with Auto Ride off or a lower Energy are quieter and less squashed.
- **A touch of head shadow.** When a pass is off to one side, the ear turned away from it hears a little less top end, so a pass feels like it goes around your head. It is subtle, and a mono fold-down is as solid as before.
- **Swirl no longer clicks.** The phaser and flanger sweeps are now smooth from sample to sample. Before, they moved in small steps about 1,500 times a second, which put a faint grit on clean, tonal sounds at high Swirl.
- **Doppler pitch like a real fly-by.** A pass now rises in pitch on the way in by more than it drops on the way out (at Doppler 100% and Bend 100%: about 9.4 semitones up, 6 down), the way a passing car or plane sounds. The total bend is the same size. High Bend settings sound more like a scream coming in and less like a dive.
- **Bend changes the pitch only.** How a pass swells and brightens is set by Doppler alone; turning Bend down no longer flattens the swell or makes the bus louder.
- **The two passes of a bus stay half a pass apart at any Chaos**, so a bus keeps a steady level (with Auto Ride off, a bed no longer dips and bumps at random as Chaos goes up). Chaos still varies each pass's length, speed, closeness and direction.
- **Low End's sub octave is steadier.** It follows the lowest part of the sound (about 40 to 120 Hz) and no longer flips at random on the noise between sounds, which was heard as rumble after a sound stopped.
- **Fill echoes are different on every track.** Each track has its own echo times (around 0.5, 1 and 2 s, in uneven ratios), so layered tracks no longer echo together on a half-second grid.
- **A new reverb in Space.** The reverb is now a smooth, dense room with no metallic ring: eight delay lines that feed each other, breathing slightly so no single note rings on. It starts about 15 ms after the sound and leaves the low end (below about 150 Hz) dry, so whooshes and hits stay clear in front of it, and it is wide while staying solid in mono. The tail grows from about 1 second to about 5 as Space goes up.
- **Space no longer leans on the limiter.** The dry sound is eased down as the room comes up (by up to about 5 dB at 100%), so the top of the knob is a wash and not just more level: at Space 100% the limiter now works about a quarter as hard on the default sounds, and the output is a little quieter there (under 1 dB). At the default 30% the level is about the same as before. The swells stay a little more distinct at the top than they were.
- **The Space echoes are less rigid.** The two ping-pong echo times are no longer in a simple ratio and each drifts by a few milliseconds, so the repeats don't settle into a fixed pattern.
- **Space at 0% is really empty.** Turning Space back up no longer plays back old echoes and reverb from before it was turned off.
- **Filter, Swirl and Low End recover sooner after a big swell.** The recent peak they measure against now falls back over about one pass (it follows Rate), not over a fixed few seconds.
- **The same feel at every sample rate and buffer size.** The glides behind the knobs, the ducking and the level matching are now timed in milliseconds, not in processing steps.
- The doppler delay is worked out at full precision, which removes a trace of noise on long, slow passes at high sample rates.
- **Cleaner pitched-up samples.** A track playing faster than it was recorded (Pitch or Semitones up, or a 96 kHz file in a 48 kHz session) no longer folds its high end back down as harsh, off-pitch tones: what would go above the top of the range is filtered out. Bright, hissy sounds pitched far up are quieter than they were, because that fold-back was most of their level. A sound playing at its own speed is sample-for-sample the file, and slightly brighter than before when it plays a little off speed.
- **Start flags are saved.** A track's start flag now comes back with the session and with the plug-in's state in a project. Loading a new file onto a track, or removing the track, puts the flag back at the top, and Cmd+Z (Ctrl+Z on Windows) brings it back along with the sound.
- **Auto gain respects peaks.** The automatic loudness trim no longer lifts peaky sounds (hits, clicks) over full scale: it stops when the loudest peak reaches 0 dBFS. Such tracks come in quieter than before; steadier sounds are levelled as they were.
- **Every added file is accounted for.** The status line says how many files went in, how many didn't fit ("2 didn't fit (ten tracks at most)") and which couldn't be read, and tells you when only the first 10 minutes of a long file were loaded ("Loaded the first 10:00 of 23:41"). Status messages use plain words throughout ("Added", "Removed").
- **Pasting between sample rates is clean.** Copying from a file at one sample rate and pasting into a track at another converts the audio with the same filtered resampler the tracks play through.
- **Safer sample swaps.** Sample data that has been replaced is kept until playback has really let go of it, even if the audio engine is paused or bypassed at that moment.
- **True-peak limiting.** The limiter now reads the waveform between the samples as well as at them, so a take stays under the Ceiling when it is converted to another sample rate or format, or played through a converter. On noisy hits the level between the samples used to reach about 3.5 dB over full scale with the Ceiling at −0.3 dB; it now stays at the Ceiling. The limiter's delay is 2 ms (it was 1.5 ms), still constant and reported to the host.
- **The limiter lets go to suit the sound.** A short over, such as a hit, is let go within a few hundredths of a second, so it no longer pulls the whoosh down behind it for a tenth of a second or more. Reduction that lasts is held steadily and let go over about a second, so a loud swell keeps more of its shape at high Push and heavy bass stays cleaner.
- **Inflate comes before Push.** Inflate now works on the mix at its own level and Push drives the result into the limiter, so Inflate does the same thing wherever Push is. The default sound is as loud as before (within 0.2 LU, as are the factory presets).
- **Meters you can read.** GR and Out have dB marks, fall back at the same speed whatever the buffer size, and keep a line at the deepest recent reduction and the highest recent peak. The GR number shows the deepest recent reduction, so a short hit is not missed. Out shows the level going to tape, before Trim, so turning your monitoring down no longer makes a take look quiet.
- **Cleaner monitoring at a high Sample Rate.** When the tape's Sample Rate is above the device's, what you hear (and what your DAW gets) is now filtered before it is brought down to the device rate, so the extra high end no longer folds back into the audible range, and it is held to the Ceiling again afterwards. The reported delay stays exact. The recorded file was always clean and is unchanged.
